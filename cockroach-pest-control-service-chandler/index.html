What to have ready before you call

You don’t need to identify the exact species. Just share what you can see and where it appeared. It can help to have these details on hand:

  • The rooms or areas where you noticed activity.
  • Whether you’ve seen a single insect or repeated activity.
  • Any visible signs near food storage, sinks, or other parts of the property.
  • Whether the space is a home or a business.
  • Any recent changes to the area that you think may matter.

Photos can help if you can take one without getting close to the insect. Don’t put yourself at risk or disturb a suspected hiding place to get a better look.

Common missteps to avoid

Try not to treat every room with a different product before asking for guidance. Using products in ways that aren’t stated on their labels can be unsafe, and it may make it harder to understand what was happening before treatment. Avoid relying on a single sighting location as proof that activity is limited to that spot.

It’s also helpful not to wait until you’ve gathered a perfect explanation. Share what you know, including uncertainty, and let the technician ask follow-up questions. A straightforward account of the signs and their locations is more useful than trying to diagnose the cause yourself.
